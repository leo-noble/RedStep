# AI use declaration

Project: Redstep: Marswalk Planner
Challenge: NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026
Last reviewed: 13 November 2026

We used generative AI tools while building Redstep, for parts of the coding and the writing. The team made the project decisions, did the source research and testing, and is responsible for what we submitted. This page says where AI contributed and where a person had to check the work, so judges, reviewers and users can see it for themselves.

## How we used AI

We used AI tools to explore implementation approaches, draft and revise code, and explain technical concepts we hadn't met before. When something broke, we used them to investigate errors, review possible fixes and suggest performance improvements. They also helped us structure and refine the technical documentation, explanatory text and project descriptions, and brainstorm how to present the project and its features.

We treated every AI suggestion as a proposal. Some we used, some we edited before integrating, and some we rejected.

## What the team is responsible for

The team chose Redstep's goals, features, user experience and technical direction, and we are responsible for its design, implementation, integration and final presentation.

Code that AI suggested or revised was reviewed and tested as part of development. We did not accept every suggestion unchanged.

Scientific claims, mission references and source data should be checked against the primary sources listed in the application and on its [Sources page](https://redstep.vercel.app/sources).

Route-planning outputs come from the algorithms, datasets and assumptions we implemented and configured. They should not be treated as verified instructions for a real mission.

We are accountable for the errors, limitations and decisions in the submitted project, whether or not AI was involved.

## AI in the running application

Redstep generates its route assessments and guidance with rules, calculations and data-processing logic that we implemented. The application does not rely on a generative AI model to decide whether a route is GO, CAUTION or NO-GO, or to produce its route-planning verdicts. We used AI while developing Redstep, which is a different thing from the live application being AI powered.

## Data, imagery and scientific integrity

We did not use AI tools as a source of scientific measurements or in place of mission datasets. Planetary maps, terrain products, rover information and other scientific inputs are attributed in the project's source documentation.

An AI-generated explanation or coding suggestion is not scientific evidence. For any statement about Mars, a mission, a measurement or a safety-related assumption, the reference is the cited dataset, publication or mission source, even where an AI tool helped draft the wording.

Any AI-generated visual or audio material used in a future version or in promotional media should be identified separately, and must not be presented as authentic NASA, JPL, USGS, ESA or rover imagery.

## Limitations

AI tools can produce incorrect code, outdated information, misleading explanations and claims that sound plausible but have no support. Human review lowers these risks but does not guarantee the project is free of errors. Redstep is an educational and research prototype, not an operational mission-planning system.
